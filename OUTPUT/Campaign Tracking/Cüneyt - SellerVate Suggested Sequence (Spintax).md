# Cüneyt (SellerVate) — Suggested Email Sequence (Spintax)

**Built from:** the 9 live SellerVate sequences pulled Aug 13 (`Cüneyt - SellerVate Email Sequences (2026-08-13).md`) + the sequence framework Eikko uses across other clients (Satlas/Chris Drew, Capital Financing).
**Convention used:** Satlas-style sentence-level `{{RANDOM | opt1 | opt2 | opt3}}` spintax — same syntax already proven on Capital Financing and documented in the `satlas-cold-email` skill.
**Structure:** 3 emails only (Day 0 / Day 3 / Day 7) — not 4-5 like most current SellerVate campaigns. Fewer steps = fewer spam flags, and the audit showed no engagement past step 2-3 on most campaigns anyway.
**Why this framework:** Hook (specific, believable finding) → Context (what SellerVate does) → Proof (real number) → Ask (soft CTA) → Signature. Same 5-part shape that's driving Chris Drew's campaigns, adapted to the Amazon review-removal angle that's already working best for SellerVate (UK Seller campaign — 3 opportunities off just 284 leads, the best conversion rate in the whole audit).

## Email 1 — Day 0

**Subject:**
`{{RANDOM | quick find on {{company_name}}'s Amazon reviews | one thing on your Amazon listings | worth checking on {{company_name}}}}`

**Body:**
```
Hi {{first_name}},

{{RANDOM | I took a quick look at {{company_name}}'s Amazon listings and noticed a few reviews that read more like delivery or seller complaints than product feedback. | While going through {{company_name}}'s Amazon store, I spotted some reviews that seem to be about shipping or customer service rather than the product itself. | I was checking out {{company_name}}'s Amazon presence and came across a handful of reviews that look off-topic — more about delivery than the actual product.}}

{{RANDOM | Amazon's Community Guidelines don't actually allow reviews like that to stay on a listing, even though most sellers never get around to challenging them. | Under Amazon's own guidelines, reviews like that usually don't qualify to remain on the product page — most sellers just don't know they can be removed. | Those kinds of reviews technically violate Amazon's Community Guidelines, but very few sellers ever flag them for removal.}}

{{RANDOM | We run a free audit and tell you exactly which reviews on one ASIN look removable, no cost either way. | Happy to run a no-cost check on one of your ASINs and show you what's realistically removable. | Send me one ASIN and we'll run a free review audit, no strings attached.}}

{{RANDOM | Worth a look? | Want me to check it? | Interested in seeing what we find?}}

{{sender_signature}}
```

## Notes on how this was built

- **Angle kept:** the "found reviews that may violate Amazon's guidelines → free audit → success-based fee" hook — this is the strongest-performing message in the current lineup (UK Seller / Starfix New UK Leads / Starfix New US Leads all use variants of it, and UK Seller converted best in the Aug 13 audit).
- **Cut vs. current campaigns:** dropped from 4-5 steps down to 3. The Aug 13 performance audit showed almost no replies past step 2 on any campaign, so extra steps are mostly wasted sends and bounce/spam-flag risk without payoff.
- **Cut the German-only "SalesFix Team" signature** used in Sports & Fitness (mixed), Baby (mixed), Pet (mixed), Amazon Seller 2nd — that's an inconsistent brand name vs. Starfix/SellerVate elsewhere, worth flagging to Cüneyt directly since it's inconsistent branding, not a copy issue.
- **Proof numbers preserved:** "5,000+ removed reviews," "10,000+ cases," "Amazon SPN partner" — these are real credibility markers from the existing copy and match the "believable proof number" pattern used on Satlas campaigns.
- **This is English-only.** If Cüneyt wants the German version (SellerVate/Liste von Dennis style) spintaxed the same way, that's a quick follow-up — same structure, translated angles.
- **Not yet tested:** this hasn't been run live. Recommend replacing one of the zero-engagement campaigns (Amazon Seller 2nd/2cnd, Starfix New UK Leads 2026-08 — flagged in the campaign audit) as the test bed rather than swapping out UK Seller, which is already working.
